# BOFH: Encounter with Ghidra

**Date: April 2025**  
**Episode: A**

It was 2 PM on a Tuesday when Simon, the BOFH (Bastard Operator From Hell), was enjoying the quiet hum of the server room. The lusers had been particularly bothersome today, their tickets piling up like digital refuse. He'd just finished implementing a creative email filter that sent all messages containing the phrase "urgent request" directly to /dev/null, when the door to his sanctum opened.

"Excuse me, is this IT?" asked a voice that didn't immediately trigger Simon's luser-detection algorithms.

Simon swiveled his chair around, preparing his standard glare, but paused when he saw her. She wore black jeans and a hoodie with a strange octopus-like logo on it.

"Depends who's asking," Simon replied, his standard acidic tone intact.

"I'm Ghidra," she said, not bothering to extend her hand. "New security analyst. I've been asked to do a vulnerability assessment of your systems." Her eyes swept over his workspace with thinly veiled judgment.

Simon's eyebrows shot up. "Ghidra? Like the NSA reverse engineering tool?"

"Yes, congratulations on recognizing a basic security reference," she said with a smirk. "I assume that's your first achievement of the day?"

Simon's expression remained neutral. "Well, most security analysts I've met couldn't tell a buffer overflow from a coffee overflow."

Ghidra pulled up a chair, uninvited. "And most sysadmins I've met think having a background in computing means they once successfully installed Windows." She glanced at his terminal. "Oh my, are you actually using TELNET in this decade? How quaint. Next you'll tell me your password is 'password' spelled with zeros instead of Os."

"Telnet remains useful for legacy systems," Simon replied evenly. "Not everything needs a shiny new protocol with unnecessary overhead."

"Since what? Since punch cards? I can practically smell the legacy systems from here." She leaned in, examining his screen. "So tell me, do you also edit JPEGs with a hex editor? Maybe keep your passwords on a Post-it note? Or did you upgrade to writing them on the whiteboard?"

Simon leaned back in his chair, unruffled. He'd seen this type before—overcompensating, desperate to prove themselves. "You know, excessive condescension is often a mask for insecurity. Found any actual vulnerabilities yet, or just rehearsing one-liners?"

Ghidra's eyes narrowed slightly. "That the last security team ran away screaming? Yes, I heard. Very impressive scaring off a bunch of CompTIA certificate holders." She pulled out her laptop. "Problem is, I'm not them. And right now your firewall has more holes than your understanding of basic cryptography."

"Error between keyboard and chair," Simon offered calmly, watching her reaction.

"Indeed," Ghidra replied coolly. "Though I suspect the chair in question is currently occupied by you." Her fingers flew across her keyboard. "By the way, that CRON job that randomly kills processes belonging to people who submit tickets with 'ASAP' in the subject line? Amateurish. Real pros make it look like a Windows update."

Simon smiled slightly. All this theater—she was working awfully hard to impress him. Interesting. "You know, if you'd found a genuine zero-day, you wouldn't be trying so hard to prove yourself with these parlor tricks."

A barely perceptible pause in her typing confirmed his suspicion.

"Oh, and I found your little backdoor. Really? Using 'drowssap' as the password? The 1990s called...." she continued, but with less conviction.

"ID-10-T error," Simon said, his voice remaining level. "But I'm curious—that latest CVE everyone's panicking about? I patched it last week. Did your team?"

Ghidra's expression flickered for just a microsecond. "But I'm here to make sure nobody else can exploit the fascinating museum of vulnerabilities you call a network." She glanced up, her expression softening slightly. "Want to help me set up a honeypot for that marketing VP who keeps downloading malware? I've got a special design that makes their cursor slowly drift to the right until they think their mouse is broken."

Simon noted the obvious attempt to change the subject. "Could be entertaining," he conceded. The games people played—always so transparent to the BOFH. He'd let her save face for now. After all, he'd been in this game long enough to recognize when someone was compensating for something. And she had potential—just needed to drop the script and do some actual work.

He tossed her an admin password. The real test was about to begin.
